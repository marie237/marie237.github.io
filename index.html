Research

I study the geometry of Teichmüller spaces using a combinatorial approach. I have also worked on CAT(0) cube complexes for asymptotically rigid mapping class groups, and on describing the topology of a chaotic attractor in the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equation through its periodic orbits.

Publications & preprints

2026Preprint

A Wolpert Theorem for shearing coordinates

arXiv:2611.05045

arXiv
Abstract

A theorem from Wolpert bounds the Weil–Petersson distance from a hyperbolic surface to a maximally noded surface in terms of the lengths of the underlying pants decomposition. We prove analogues for punctured surfaces both in terms of the lambda-lengths and in terms of the shearing coordinates of an ideal triangulation.

2026Preprint

Hexagon decompositions and the Weil–Petersson metric

arXiv:2609.31278

arXiv
Abstract

We give an explicit quasi-isometry from the flip graph of triangulations Fg,n to the ε-thick part of Teichmüller space equipped with the Teichmüller metric, by mapping each ideal triangulation to the hyperbolic surface whose shearing coordinates along that triangulation are all equal to zero. Extending this construction, we obtain a quasi-isometry Q from the hexagon graph Hg,n to the augmented Teichmüller space Teich¯g,n equipped with the Weil-Petersson metric, and we estimate its width, that is, the Hausdorff distance between Q(Hg,n) and Teich¯g,n. By bounding the Weil-Petersson distance along grafting rays, and by using an analogue for ideal triangulations of Wolpert's upper bound on the Weil-Petersson distance to a stratum, we show that the width is at most of order (g+n)log(g+n). We also provide an explicit projection from any point in Teich¯g,n to the thick part of boundary strata, maintaining simultaneous control over the Weil-Petersson distance and the shearing coordinates.

2025Preprint

Bounding shears of spiralling triangulations on hyperbolic surfaces

arXiv:2512.09818 · Revised September 2026

arXiv
Abstract

We show that all hyperbolic surfaces admit an ideal triangulation with bounded shear parameters. This upper bound depends logarithmically on the topology of the surface.

2025Published

The topology of a chaotic attractor in the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equation

With Pierre Beck, Jeremy P. Parker & Tobias M. Schneider

Chaos 35, 013123 (2025)

Journal arXiv
Abstract

The Birman-Williams theorem gives a connection between the collection of unstable periodic orbits (UPOs) contained within a chaotic attractor and the topology of that attractor, for three-dimensional systems. In certain cases, the fractal dimension of a chaotic attractor in a partial differential equation (PDE) is less than three, even though that attractor is embedded within an infinite-dimensional space. Here we study the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky PDE at the onset of chaos. We use two different dimensionality-reduction techniques — proper orthogonal decomposition and an autoencoder neural network — to find two different mappings of the chaotic attractor into three dimensions. By finding the image of the attractor's UPOs in these reduced spaces and examining their linking numbers, we construct templates for the branched manifold which encodes the topological properties of the attractor. The templates obtained using two different dimensionality reduction methods are equivalent. The organization of the periodic orbits is identical and consistent symbolic sequences for low-period UPOs are derived. While this is not a formal mathematical proof, this agreement is strong evidence that the dimensional reduction is robust, in this case, and that an accurate topological characterization of the chaotic attractor of the chaotic PDE has been achieved.

2025Published

CAT(0) cube complexes and asymptotically rigid mapping class groups

This work comes from the first part of my master’s thesis supervised by Christian Urech at EPFL. I also did a semester project under his supervision.

Journal of Group Theory 28(4), 811–833 (2025)

Journal arXiv
Abstract

In this article we study the asymptotically rigid mapping class groups of infinitely-punctured surfaces obtained by thickening planar trees. We present a family of CAT(0) cube complexes on which the latter groups act. Along the way, we determine in which cases the cube complexes introduced earlier by Genevois, Lonjou and Urech are CAT(0).
